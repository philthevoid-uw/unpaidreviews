How I review

Trust is the only thing this site offers, so the rules are written down and apply to every review.

Independence rules

  • I pay my own way. Products are bought at normal retail price. Meals, hotels and tours are paid in full.
  • No payment based on purchases. There are no affiliate links, commissions or referral fees. Neither the site nor its owners earn anything when you click or buy something reviewed here.
  • No revenue incentive over coverage. No revenue incentive influences what is reviewed or not reviewed, or whether a review is positive or negative.
  • Community reviews follow the same policy. Any review sourced from the community must adhere to these rules, and the community is built on these guiding principles.
  • No sponsored content, gifts, comps or press trips. If something is given to me without being asked (a free dessert, an upgrade), I say so at the top of the review.
  • Disclosure on every review. Each page states exactly how the item was obtained.
  • No conflicts of interest. I do not review businesses owned by or employing people I know. If a relationship exists, I skip the review or disclose it.
  • Associations minimized. I keep any association with a reviewed business, brand or sponsor to a minimum so that no revenue stream can influence a review, and I disclose any that remain.
  • All revenue sources disclosed. Every way the site earns money is listed on how the site is funded.
  • No pre-publication previews. Businesses never see a review before it goes live, and cannot change an opinion or a score.

How the site pays for itself is covered on a separate page: how the site is funded.

How scoring works

Each review rates four criteria from 1 to 10. The criteria depend on the category:

CategoryCriteria
ProductsPerformance, Value, Durability, Ease of use
TravelExperience, Value, Getting around, Crowds and authenticity
RestaurantsFood, Service, Atmosphere, Value
WineAroma, Palate, Finish, Value

The overall score is a considered judgment, not a mechanical average, and the review explains it.

Wine quick notes: older bottles from my CellarTracker history appear as short quick notes with a score and, where I wrote one, a tasting note. CellarTracker uses a 100-point scale, so a 90 there is shown here as 9.0 out of 10. Quick notes have no criteria breakdown, and each one says how the bottle was acquired, or that I no longer have a record.

Scale: 9–10 exceptional · 7–8 good · 5–6 mixed · 3–4 poor · 1–2 avoid.

Testing standards

  • Products: used for a meaningful period before publishing, with the duration stated and at least one alternative compared where possible.
  • Travel: visited in person, with the dates and season stated and costs given in what I actually paid.
  • Wine: tasted with the bottle's vintage, producer and price noted, with how long it was open and how it changed where I tracked that.
  • Restaurants: visited at least twice where practical, ordering and paying like any customer, without announcing that I write reviews.

Every review has the same parts

Bottom line, who it is for, how I tested or visited, scores, what worked, flaws but not dealbreakers, and what I would skip. Using one format makes reviews comparable and makes it harder to hide a weak spot.

Updates and corrections

Reviews are dated and updated when things change. Errors are corrected in the open: see corrections.
