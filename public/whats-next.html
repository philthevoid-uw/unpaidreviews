Reader reviews: the plan

Draft. Nothing here is live yet.

For now, every review is written by one person. The plan is to open the site to reader reviews once the foundation is solid. The community will be built on these guiding principles:

  • Reviews from real experience. Readers will say how they used the product or visited the place.
  • Same policy as the site. Every community review must follow the site policy: no one is paid based on purchases of what is reviewed, and no revenue incentive influences what is reviewed or whether it is positive or negative.
  • No incentives. Nobody can offer, request or reward a review.
  • Businesses cannot edit or buy their way out. They may respond publicly to a review, and the review stays.
  • Transparent moderation. Published rules, and a regular report on what was removed and why.
  • Reader and editor ratings stay separate. My scores will never be blended into the community score.

Lessons from wine communities

Wine sites such as CellarTracker and Vivino have run large reader communities for years. Here is what I plan to borrow and what I plan to do differently.

  • Notes tied to a real bottle. A review says which vintage was opened, how it was stored and served, and how it was acquired. One bottle's flaw does not make another reader's review wrong.
  • Handles, not real names. Readers choose a public handle. Real names stay private unless the reader chooses to show them.
  • Comments that notify. When someone replies to a comment, the original commenter is told. Community members on existing sites say missing reply alerts are a common frustration.
  • Follow a taster, not a score. Readers can follow people whose palates match theirs, as on CellarTracker and Vivino, instead of relying on one blended number.
  • No scores from too few voices. A community average appears only after enough independent ratings, with the count always shown.
  • No republished professional scores. Critics' points and notes are copyrighted, so they are not copied here.
  • Your own words only. Notes written by AI or copied from elsewhere are not allowed.
  • Community kept apart from selling. There are no shop links, affiliate links or purchase-based payments in reviews or in the community, so nothing pushes readers toward buying.
  • Cellar tools stay elsewhere. Inventory, bin locations and drinking windows are better handled by CellarTracker, so this site covers published reviews only.
